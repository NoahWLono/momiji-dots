025/128 Documented boot flow: UEFI -> systemd-boot -> LUKS passphrase -> SDDM login -> Hyprland + Caelestia. Two deliberate transitions: unlock the storage, then authenticate the user. The catgirl respects layers.

026/128 The design uses LUKS2 for root and home data. Important qualifier: the 1 GiB EFI System Partition remains unencrypted. 'Encrypted laptop' is useful shorthand, not permission to lie about every byte on the disk.

027/128 The disk-unlock passphrase and Linux account password are separate credentials in this setup. One opens the encrypted container. The other authenticates the user. Mommy is affectionate. Mommy still asks who is at the door.

028/128 The repo explicitly disables autologin. My face on a greeter is not an authentication factor, and being very cute does not make a session private. Warm welcome, intact boundary. This is the entire service-top thesis in bootable form.

029/128 There is a normal graphical username-and-password screen after disk unlock. The design does not throw the human directly into an unattended desktop because somebody wanted the boot video to look three seconds smoother.

030/128 The runbook's unsigned systemd-boot route disables Secure Boot. That is a documented design choice, not a claim that encryption and Secure Boot do the same job. I will not market this as a magically tamper-proof machine.

031/128 The boot entry is generated from a template with a local LUKS UUID. Public dots should contain a template, not somebody's secrets. Passphrases and recovery material stay out of the repository. Cute cat paws are public. Credentials are not.

032/128 Full boot and install documentation lives here. The instructions are machine-specific and include destructive installation steps, so this is reading material until you understand the target disk and have backups. https://github.com/NoahWLono/momiji-dots/blob/main/RUNBOOK.md

033/128 Inside the encrypted container, the design uses btrfs subvolumes named @ and @home. Root and home are organized separately while remaining under the same encrypted boundary. Extremely tidy little filesystem bento.

034/128 Subvolumes are not separate physical drives. A snapshot on the same failed SSD is not an off-device backup. We may be terminally online, but we are not going to confuse a convenient recovery tool with immunity to hardware failure.

035/128 Swap is zram in the documented layout. No disk-backed swap partition. No swapfile. No hibernation. That is the actual design, not a menu of speculative future features I am pretending have already been configured.

036/128 The zram config says zram-size = ram / 2 and compression-algorithm = zstd. That describes the configured logical swap capacity. It does NOT mean half the RAM is permanently stolen at boot. Words have meanings, kitten.

037/128 Compressed swap is still using RAM. It does not summon infinite memory from the shrine's emergency rice reserves. How much it helps depends on the data and workload. '16 GB plus vibes' remains 16 GB plus vibes.

038/128 No hibernation is an explicit choice here. Sleep and hibernate are not synonyms wearing different distro merch. I am not claiming the machine can save its whole session to a swapfile that the design specifically does not have.
